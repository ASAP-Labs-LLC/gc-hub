**v3.0.0: range-driven deviation bullets and sample comments.** The
deviation report on the Analysis tab, the exported analysis PDF and the PDF
attached to QBench **read differently**: one bullet per range that deviates
plus at most one for everything outside the ranges, instead of one bullet per
excursion. Operators no longer edit the bullets; they add **comments**, which
are saved on the sample and printed in every report. No D2887/D86 number, the
results CSV, the dashboard numbers or Export to LIMS change.

**The new thresholds were tuned on synthetic data** (a same-product diesel
pair and diesel with 1–5% gasoline, modelled on the real calibration axis;
no real sample/standard pair was available). Before relying on the bullets
for customer reports, run the Analysis tab on real runs you already know
(clean diesel against its own standard, a known gasoline-contaminated
sample) and check the bullets and the conclusion. The settings below are
admin-editable if they need adjusting.

## Changes to how reports read

- **One bullet per deviating range.** Each range overlay (by default Gas
  C5–C11 and Oil C20–C44, or the saved ranges) gets a bullet only when it
  deviates; everything outside the ranges is summarised in at most one more
  line. A report never has more lines than ranges + 1 (v1/v2 wrote dozens on
  ordinary diesel). Examples:
  - `• Gas (C5–C11): HIGHER than Diesel #2 — moderate (max +620 at 1.00 min; 20% of range beyond the marginal threshold)`
  - `• Oil (C20–C44, evaluated to C28): LOWER than Diesel #2 — significant (max -2500 at 6.50 min; 25% of range beyond the marginal threshold)` (the range runs past the x-axis limit, so only its shown part is evaluated)
  - `• Gas (C5–C11): HIGHER than Diesel #2 — marginal; also lower moderate 2.00–2.10 min (max +200 at 0.60 min; 37% of range beyond the marginal threshold)` (an opposite-direction part reaching moderate is named with its own severity)
  - `• Gas (C5–C11): HIGHER than Diesel #2 — moderate, sharp peaks only (2 sharp peaks above the standard at 0.84, 1.40 min; no broad deviation above the marginal threshold)` (gasoline-like spikes the smoothed trend cannot see)
  - `• Gas (C5–C11): mixed: LOWER than Diesel #2 overall, with 2 sharp peaks above the standard at 2.00, 2.40 min — moderate (…)`
  - `No deviations above the marginal threshold within the defined ranges.` followed by `• Outside the defined ranges: HIGHER than Diesel #2 — moderate at 3.70–3.95, 4.20–4.30, 4.60–4.70 min, +2 more (max +700 at 5.00 min)`
  - with no ranges defined: `• Across the run: HIGHER than Diesel #2 — moderate at 1.00–1.60 min (max +620 at 1.00 min)`
  - nothing deviates: `No deviations above the marginal threshold.`
  - a range outside the calibration, the run or the x-axis: `• Light (C3–C4): not evaluated — outside the evaluated window (calibration, run end or x-axis limit)`
  - no usable calibration for the sample: `• Calibration unavailable — ranges not evaluated: …`, and the conclusion says the ranges could not be evaluated.
- **How a range is judged.** The severity (marginal, moderate, significant:
  the three thresholds on the Analysis tab) and the direction (HIGHER or
  LOWER, by the larger area) come from runs of the smoothed difference that
  stay beyond the marginal threshold for at least the minimum run width,
  clipped to the range; a sharp peak counts on its own (below). Everything
  is evaluated only up to the x-axis limit, the graph actually shown. A
  single-carbon range (C7–C7) covers half a carbon each side.
- **Sharp peaks are qualified (fixes false "mixed contamination").** The
  spike channel (which catches short tall spikes such as gasoline
  adulteration, erased by the smoothed trend) now counts a peak only when it
  reaches the spike report threshold, is **sharp** (no wider than 0.20 min at
  half height) and **dominates** the local peak (the difference is at least
  0.6 × the larger of the sample's and the standard's peak there), after one
  small global alignment of the sample to the standard (±0.02 min). v1 and
  v2 counted the height and retention differences of peaks both runs share,
  so **two batches of the same diesel could be reported as "elevated in the
  gas range and the oil range … consistent with mixed contamination"**. On
  the same-product test pair v2 wrote 29 bullets and that conclusion; v3
  reports "No deviations above the marginal threshold." and a consistent
  conclusion, while diesel with 1–5% gasoline flags Gas only.
- **The conclusion** keeps v2's wording, but a range counts as elevated only
  when its bullet reports something higher (the direction, sharp peaks
  above, or a higher "also" part). It **stays editable**; an edited
  conclusion is recorded as edited (`report_log`, below).
- **Bullets are read-only.** They are computed on the server, identically
  for the Analysis tab, the direct export, the queue/ZIP export and the
  QBench upload, from the same parameters, thresholds, ranges and x-axis
  limit. The export modal shows them as a preview. v2 let a typed or stale
  bullet text override the computed one (and a direct export sent the whole
  report box, header lines included); that text is now ignored. Queued items
  keep the parameters they were queued with, and the ZIP and QBench PDFs use
  them (v2's used the saved defaults for the charts and the QBench bullets).
  Free text goes into comments.
- **The difference plot** (screen and PDF) shows dotted ±marginal/moderate/
  significant lines and marks every counted sharp peak, so each bullet can
  be checked against the graph. The PDF uses the report's x-axis limit.
- **Range boxes and carbon markers use the sample's own calibration.** The
  range boxes, the dashboard and Chromatogram-tab carbon markers and the
  annotation description use the ladder of the revision the sample was
  computed with (its instrument's calibration). v2 labelled every sample,
  including GC-2's, with GC-1's current calibration, and invented carbon
  numbers when it had none. When the Chromatogram tab overlays samples with
  different calibrations, the markers are the first visible trace's and the
  title names it.
- **Ranges.** An explicitly empty range list means no ranges everywhere
  (v2's exports fell back to Gas/Oil). Range labels are one line, at most 40
  characters.
- **The PDF footer** prints the parameters used (quantile, window,
  smoothing, the three thresholds, x-axis limit, minimum run width, merge
  gap, spike minimum width, spike report threshold, spike maximum width and
  dominance), the ranges and the app version. Every name, label, bullet and
  comment in the report is escaped.

## Comments

- **The Analysis tab has a Comments section** under the bullets: one-click
  preset chips, a free-text box and the sample's comments with their
  initials and time, each deletable after a confirmation. A comment is at
  most 500 characters; a sample holds at most 100.
- **Initials are self-declared, not authenticated.** "Your initials" (1–4
  letters) is remembered per browser and required to add or delete a
  comment. The hub also stores the requesting computer's address with each
  comment and deletion; reports print initials only.
- **Deleting is a soft delete** (who and when are kept), because comments
  feed reports.
- **Presets** are managed on the Hub admin page (`/admin/hub`, admin
  password): add, edit, reorder, deactivate (at most 50, 200 characters
  each). Seeded once with: "Sample appears to be a renewable fuel, not
  conventional petroleum diesel.", "Sample appears to be gasoline.",
  "Possible contamination; results should be interpreted with caution.",
  "Re-run requested." A comment copies the preset's text, so editing a
  preset later does not change existing comments.
- **Annotations are saved as comments.** A region marked on the trend plot
  becomes an annotation comment with its time span (default text "Marked
  region Cx–Cy" from the sample's calibration). They are drawn from the
  saved comments, survive a reload, and are never written into the bullet
  text. *Clear Annotations* asks for confirmation naming how many it will
  delete and soft-deletes only annotation comments. Queue items no longer
  carry annotations.
- **Reports print the comments.** The analysis PDF, the ZIP export and the
  QBench PDF get a *Comments* section after the bullets (omitted when there
  are none), oldest first: `text (initials, date)`, and for an annotation
  `text (Cx–Cy, a–b min; initials, date)`. **QBench PDFs may reach
  customers**: word comments accordingly.
- **`report_log`.** Every exported PDF (download, ZIP, QBench upload) is
  recorded in the database: sample and revision, standard, parameters,
  ranges, windows, the bullets, the conclusion (and whether it was edited),
  the comment ids included, the PDF's sha256, the app version and the
  requesting computer's address.

## Database and rollback

- **Schema v2**, applied automatically at the first v3 start: three new
  tables (`comment_presets`, `sample_comments`, `report_log`); nothing
  existing is changed. The database is copied to
  `data\backups\pre-migrate-1-<time>.db` first.
- **Rolling back to v2.0.0 is safe**: v2.0.0 starts on a v2 database (it
  ignores the newer tables). While it runs, its reports **omit comments** and
  use v2's per-excursion bullets, it writes **no `report_log` rows**, and
  comments cannot be added. Comments made under v3 are kept and reappear
  after upgrading again.

## New admin settings

Settings > **Deviation Bullets** (admin password; also saved by *Set as
Default*):

| Setting | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `analysis_min_width_min` | 0.05 min | a trend run must stay beyond the marginal threshold this long to count |
| `analysis_merge_gap_min` | 0.10 min | outside-range pieces closer than this are shown as one span |
| `analysis_spike_report_threshold` | empty (= the moderate threshold) | a sharp peak must reach this to count |
| `analysis_spike_max_fwhm_min` | 0.20 min | a counted peak is no wider than this at half height |
| `analysis_spike_min_dominance` | 0.6 | a counted peak's difference is at least this fraction of the local peak height |

The existing `analysis_spike_min_width_min` (0.02 min) is now also saved by
*Set as Default*. Invalid values (non-numeric, negative) are refused.

## For scripts and integrations

- `POST /api/analysis` returns `items`, `text`, `windows`, `spikes`,
  `params_used`, `conclusion`, `cal_times`/`cal_carbons`; `report` and
  `segments` are gone. Export and QBench payloads carry `ranges` and the
  trend parameters; a `bullets` field is ignored.
- `GET /api/samples/<id>/trace` adds `cal_times`/`cal_carbons` (the
  revision's ladder).
- New routes: `GET/POST /api/samples/<id>/comments`,
  `POST /api/samples/<id>/comments/<cid>/delete`, `GET /api/comment-presets`
  and admin `POST /api/admin/comment-presets`.
