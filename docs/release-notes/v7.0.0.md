**v7.0.0: findings and conclusions that follow the data, every range on the report, report charts that work in colour and in black and white, corrected D86 that never steps down, and the distillation curve on Overview.** This is a MAJOR release: corrected D86 is calculated differently for new results, and the report's wording and charts change.

## Upgrading from v6.0.1

- Install as usual with Admin · Server > "Restart & install v7.0.0".
- **No database change.** Rolling back to v6.0.1 is safe.
- **Results already stored are not changed.** Export to LIMS re-sends them as stored. Re-process a sample to apply the new corrected D86 rule (its Data view shows a note and the button when its stored series steps down).

## Findings and conclusions

- **The conclusion always agrees with the findings.** Before, a range that was lower overall but had one sharp peak above the standard was called "elevated", and a range that was only lower was left out, so a sample clearly lower in the gas range could read "consistent with the reference standard". Now each range gets one verdict, **higher**, **lower** or **mixed** (both directions reach moderate), and the finding and the conclusion both say it.
- **Plain-language findings.** Each line leads with the range and the verdict, e.g. "Gas (C5–C11): higher than Diesel #2 — moderate, light end elevated (max +620 at 1.00 min; 20% of range beyond the marginal threshold)".
- **More specific conclusions.**
  - Whether a range is the light end, main body or heavy end is judged from its carbons against the standard's own distribution, not from its name.
  - Elevated light end: "consistent with possible light-end (gasoline-range) contamination". Reduced light end: "consistent with a heavier cut or loss of light components (weathering/evaporation)". Elevated heavy end: "consistent with heavier components such as lube/oil-range material". A mixed range: "differs in shape … consistent with a different product or a blend".
  - Findings are ordered by severity. A reduced light end together with an elevated heavy end is summed up as a heavier overall distribution.
  - Sharp peaks get their own sentence ("isolated sharp peaks above the standard at …, which may indicate specific added components"), and a deviation outside the defined ranges is mentioned.
  - It still ends with "These findings are indicative only and do not confirm specific substances." The redundant "Conclusion:" prefix is gone, since the report and Compare already title the box.

## Every range on the report

- **The ranges on screen are the ranges printed**, on every path: Export report, Add to queue, Download all, Upload to QBench and the bulk actions from the Samples list. Before, switching to Overview or Data, opening another sample, or a bulk action fell back to the saved default ranges.
- Compare keeps each sample's adjustments for the browser tab. The report queue shows which ranges each item will print, and offers "Update from current view" when an unsent item's view has changed. The export sheet names every range.
- **Every range is named on the page.** Range labels appear on both the trend and the difference chart; a band too narrow for its name shows its number, and the footer lists every range (with that number). The footer no longer ends "and N more".

## Report charts

- Colour is used only where it means something, and every mark also has a texture so the page reads the same printed in black and white:
  - the sample is the only coloured line (teal, prints dark grey) over the standard's light grey area;
  - higher than the standard is red with diagonal hatching, lower is blue with dots;
  - spike triangles point up or down; threshold lines differ by dash;
  - range bands are neutral grey with hairline edges.
- The legends show the same colour and texture swatches.
- The report is still exactly one page.

## Corrected D86 never decreases

- Correction factors are applied per cut, so a negative factor could make a corrected D86 temperature fall below an earlier one (most often T10 below T5), and earlier versions reported it that way. From v7.0.0 the **corrected** D86 (written to the results file LEM reads, exported, and shown with Corrected D86 on) holds each cut at no less than the cut before it, from IBP to FBP, 40% and 60% included.
- The **uncorrected** D86 (Corrected D86 off) and the D2887 temperatures are unchanged and still show any dip, so the plain conversion stays visible.
- In the Data view, held values are marked `*` with a note.
- In v1's production history, 38 of 80 rows would change, mostly blanks at FBP plus a few samples at T10. The v1 parity check records this as a documented, proven difference.

## Distillation curve on Overview

- The Overview tab now shows the distillation curve: D86 (following the Corrected D86 switch) and D2887, told apart by colour and line style, with a dot at each percent point.
- Hover, tap or tab to a dot to see its temperature in a callout ("50% recovered · 285.1 °C"); the matching row in the Results card lights up, and hovering a Results row lights up its dot.
- Works for imported v1 results too, follows the theme, and fits a phone screen.
