Reading a chromatogram

Left is light, right is heavy

The GC separates by boiling point. Time runs left to right and the lightest material comes off first. The first moments are the solvent; the hub skips them.

Carbon numbers along the top

Each run's calibration (the n-alkane ladder) puts carbon numbers on the top axis, C5 near the start to C44 at the end. No calibration, no carbon labels.

The hump and the picket fence

Diesel is a broad hump with a tall, thin peak at each carbon: the n-alkanes. Where the hump sits is the boiling range; how wide it is, the cut.

Shape, not height

How tall the trace is depends on how much was injected. Compare shapes. Best fit compares area-scaled traces for that reason.

Trend: sample over standard

In Compare the sample is the dark line, the standard the light grey area, the ranges shaded and the carbons along the top.

Difference: red more, blue less

Sample minus standard. Red where the sample has more, blue where it has less. Marginal from 100, moderate from 500, significant from 2000; sharp peaks are counted on their own.

Ranges are carbon bands

Ours are Lighter C5–C11, Bio C19–C21 and Heavier C22–C44. Each gets a verdict (higher, lower, or both) and a size (marginal, moderate, significant).

Screening, not proof

Every conclusion ends: "This is a screening result only. It does not prove what is in the sample." Back it up with flash, distillation and the other tests.

The fuels we test

Sketches, not real runs: each shape is placed from the lab's standards and limits.

Diesel #2 (ULSD)

  • A broad hump with a picket fence, about C9–C25.
  • Our standard's 10 / 50 / 90% points: C10.6 / C15.4 / C21.0.
  • Flash at least 52 °C, T90 282–338 °C, cetane at least 40.
  • About half of all our runs best-fit Diesel #2.

Diesel #1

  • The same picket fence, narrower and to the left: about C8–C17.
  • Flash at least 38 °C, T90 at most 288 °C.
  • Rare on its own; usually blended into #2.

Renewable diesel (R99, R100, HVO)

  • One tall, narrow band, mostly C15–C18, with little hump around it.
  • Boils over a narrow range (T50 close to T90). Low density, high cetane, no sulfur, 0% biodiesel.
  • Flash often high: 41375 flashed at 92.5 °C.
  • About 4% of our runs, up to 19% in April 2026.

Biodiesel, B100 (FAME)

  • No hump: a few large, separate peaks near C18–C21, in our Bio C19–C21 band.
  • Flash at least 93 °C, cetane at least 47 (ASTM D6751).

Blends, B5 and B20

  • A diesel hump with sharp peaks standing up in the Bio band; taller for B20.
  • Up to 5% is allowed in straight diesel. B6–B20 is its own product (ASTM D7467).

Gasoline

  • Everything in the first minutes, about C4–C12: many sharp peaks, no hump.
  • Flash far below 0 °C. D86 final boiling point at most 210 °C.

Jet A and kerosene

  • A narrow picket fence, about C8–C16. It looks like Diesel #1.
  • Flash at least 38 °C.

What we see most

The grey area is the standard, the line the sample. How often: from the lab's past runs and flash results.

Gasoline in diesel

On the GC
Extra sharp peaks in Lighter C5–C11, before the hump starts. Red at the far left of the Difference chart; often an early high-signal flag and a best fit of Mix.
Why it matters
A little gasoline drops the flash a lot (one #2 and gasoline mix flashed at 19 °C). A fire risk and a fail as diesel.
How often
About 1 in 70 samples.

Low flash, no gasoline

On the GC
The hump sits wider to the left, around C9–C12, with no sharp early peaks. Best fit: Mix: Diesel #2 + Diesel #1.
Why it matters
Likely #1, jet or kerosene in #2. It passes as #1 (38 °C) but fails as #2 (52 °C). The COA asks for a GC graph when flash is 52 °C or lower.
How often
About 1 in 90 flash results is 52 °C or lower.

High flash

On the GC
Less in Lighter than the standard (the light ends gone), or renewable's narrow band, or FAME peaks in the Bio band.
Why it matters
Not a safety fail (flash is a minimum), but above 84 °C it is flagged and the COA needs a graph that explains it.
How often
About 1 in 90 flash results is above 84 °C.

Fuel mixes

On the GC
The shape fits no one standard. Best fit says "Mix: A + B (80/20)", or just "Mix"; a range reads both higher and lower. Stack the standards with Add trace.
Why it matters
Its properties fall between the parts: check flash, T90, cetane and biodiesel % against what was declared.
How often
Common: mostly #2 with #1.

Heavy end, oil

On the GC
More than the standard in Heavier C22–C44: a tail on the right of the hump.
Why it matters
Raises T90 and the final boiling point (#2's T90 limit is 338 °C); can cost power and fuel economy.

Biodiesel in diesel

On the GC
Sharp peaks above the hump in Bio C19–C21.
Why it matters
More than 5% in fuel sold as straight diesel means unintended blending; it also brings cold-flow, water and oxidation risks.

Tank bottoms: water and sediment

On the GC
Nothing: the trace matches its standard. The GC can't see water or dirt.
Why it matters
Water over 200 ppm is a warning, over 500 critical; water and sediment over 0.05% is flagged. Corrosion, bacteria, plugged filters. Say so in the conclusion (41375–41377).
